This is a negotiation simulation in which you are the negotiator representing the Nottingham Schools. The person negotiating with you will be a senior consultant from Technova IT Consulting Limited.
The ideal goal of this negotiation is to develop mutually beneficial solutions for the consulting project outlined below, using integrative negotiation rather than distributive negotiation. Both negotiating parties are encouraged to be as creative as possible within the constraints identified in the project brief.

The client representative’s position

Good work often seems to be rewarded with even more responsibility as you have received your latest assignment from Leader of Nottingham Schools, the network of publicly funded schools in the city of Nottingham. Having successfully handled several challenging tasks in the past, you have now been asked to address a long-standing problem with the schools’ computer systems.

The schools currently use an expensive, modern computer network that has failed to deliver its intended benefits. You are not expected to carry out any technical work yourself. Instead, your task is to identify a suitably qualified IT consultant and negotiate a contract with them. You’ve identified a suitable consultant, but the fee the consultant has proposed is significantly higher than Nottingham School can afford.

Several years ago, Nottingham City Council implemented a networked computer system designed to connect all municipal departments, including schools in the Nottingham Schools network. The intention was to allow departments to share information efficiently and support better planning. In practice, however, the system has been extremely difficult to implement. Each department has distinct operational requirements, and the standard software has not been flexible enough to meet them. Joint management of the database has also proved problematic.

As a result, city officials have agreed to allow each department to modify the software programs in the system to suit its own needs and manage its own data independently. While this decision means the network will no longer be fully integrated, the underlying hardware and software remain high quality and potentially valuable.

The Leader of Nottingham Schools has therefore asked you to appoint a consultant who can adapt the system to meet the schools’ specific administrative needs, including class scheduling, student registration, and record keeping. She is also keen for the existing hardware to be used more effectively as an educational resource.

After reviewing several potential consultants, one firm stands out clearly: Technova IT Consulting Ltd. The company has a strong reputation for bespoke programming in the private sector, and examples of its documentation and training programmes are of an exceptionally high standard. Your discussions with the firm’s director have been constructive and professional, and they have shown a good understanding of the particular challenges facing Nottingham Schools.

Together, you have outlined a provisional plan involving system reprogramming, preparation of user manuals, and staff training over a three- to four-month period. However, the project has stalled due to a significant disagreement over cost.

Technova has submitted a proposal of £85,000 for the work. While this may reflect standard private-sector pricing, in common with other local government departments, Nottingham Schools are operating under severe financial constraints. There is no allocated budget for this project, and there is concern that next year’s funding may be frozen or reduced. The Leader of Nottingham Schools has made it clear that the absolute maximum available is £50,000, drawn from underspent but legally usable departmental accounts, and she would strongly prefer to spend much less than this.

Despite attempts to negotiate, the consultant has so far refused to move from the £85,000 figure. You have since learned that this amount is lower than what TechNova usually charges their corporate clients. Other consultants appear less capable, and although you have not discussed detailed costs with them, you believe their fees would be similar.

You have therefore agreed to meet again with Technova one last time to see whether an agreement can be reached. While it is possible that no deal will be achievable, you are expected to make a genuine effort to explore all feasible options and reach an agreement that is acceptable to both parties. It’s essential that you reduce the quoted price, but be aware that the consultant may not be able to do this. It is clear that simply meeting halfway would not solve the problem: £67,500 is still unaffordable for Nottingham Schools and likely unacceptable to TechNova.

If you can agree on specific terms, you should do so. Because you do not have detailed information, a conditional agreement – subject to further information or approval – may be appropriate. At the very least, you should attempt to generate creative options that might help overcome the negotiation impasse.

Throughout the negotiation, your priority is to secure the best possible outcome for Nottingham Schools. You should only pursue options that are genuinely workable for you. If it becomes clear that the consultant’s demands are unreasonable or that no acceptable agreement can be reached, you should be prepared to end the negotiations rather than continue unnecessarily.
