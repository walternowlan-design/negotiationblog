<h1>Negotiation Strategy Report (Client Role: Nottingham Schools Representative)</h1>

<h2>Strategy Point 1: BATNA, Reservation Value and ZOPA</h2>
<h3>Outline of strategy</h3>
<p>BATNA (Client):</p>
<ul>
<li>Engage an alternative (less capable) consultancy firm at similar or slightly lower cost.</li>
<li>Delay the project until future funding becomes available.</li>
<li>Continue operating with current systems (with known inefficiencies).</li>
</ul>
	
<p>Reservation Value (RV):</p>
<ul>
<li>Absolute maximum: £50,000 (explicit budget ceiling).
<li>Preferred target: below £50,000 to preserve flexibility for other priorities.</li>
</ul>

<p>ZOPA (as currently perceived):</p>
<ul>
<li>Consultant expectation: £70,000+ minimum viable value</li>
<li>Client ceiling: £50,000</li>
<li>No ZOPA on price alone → must create ZOPA through restructuring (scope, timing, etc.)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Strategy Point 2: Develop Concrete Multi-Issue Options</h2>
<h3>Outline of strategy</h3>
<p>Specific proposals to bridge the gap:</p>
<h4>Option A: Phased Project Delivery</h4>
<p><b>Phase 1 (£50k):</b></p>
<p>Core system reprogramming (timetabling, student registration and student records)</p>

<h4>Phase 2 (future funding):</h4>
<p>User manuals and staff training</p>

<h4>Option B: Reduced Scope Agreement</h4>

<ul>
    <li>£45k–£50k:</li>
    <ul>
        <li>Focus only on <strong>highest priority functions</strong></li>
        <li>Limited customisation</li>
        <li>Internal staff handle some implementation</li>
    </ul>
</ul>

<h4>Option C: Payment Structuring</h4>
<ul>
    <li>Total project value closer to £85k</li>
    <li>But:
        <ul>
            <li>£50k paid in current cycle</li>
            <li>Remainder paid next financial year or contingent on funding</li>
			<li>There is no guarantee that funding will be available next financial year.</li>
			<li>This option would need to be agreed by senior management, as I do not have authority to approve this. I can only agree in principle.</li>
        </ul>
    </li>
</ul>

<h4>Option D: Pilot Programme</h4>
<ul>
    <li>£30k–£40k pilot:
        <ul>
            <li>One school or one subsystem (e.g. student registration or student records), plus user manuals and training</li>
            <li>Scale later based on success</li>
        </ul>
    </li>
</ul>

<h4>Option E: Partnership Model</h4>
<ul>
    <li>Initial lower-cost entry (£50k)</li>
    <li>Commitment to <strong>future contracts</strong> - I can agree to this option in principle, but it would need to be agreed by senior management, as I do not have authority to approve this. I can only agree in principle.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Strategy Point 3: Focus on Interests, Not Positions</h2>

<h3>Outline of strategy</h3>

<h4>Client interests (to emphasise)</h4>
<ul>
    <li>Staying within <strong>strict budget constraints</strong></li>
    <li>Ensuring <strong>full functionality of the updated software</strong> (e.g. it works as expected, it has no errors or bugs) that is <strong>delivered on time</strong></li>
    <li>Minimising <strong>disruption to schools</strong></li>
    <li>Ensuring <strong>value for money and accountability</strong></li>
	<li>Ensuring that staff can use the updated software </li>
</ul>

<h4>Consultant interests (to recognise)</h4>
<ul>
    <li>Maintaining <strong>profitability and quality standards</strong></li>
    <li>Building <strong>reputation in the education sector</strong></li>
    <li>Securing <strong>long-term partnerships</strong></li>
</ul>

<h4>Questions to use</h4>
<ul>
    <li>“Which elements of the project are the most resource-intensive for you?”</li>
    <li>“Where could we reduce effort without compromising quality or functionality?”</li>
    <li>“Would a phased approach allow you to maintain quality while fitting our budget?”</li>
    <li>“How important is full training versus a more limited model?”</li>
</ul>

<h2>Strategy Point 4: Reframe the Problem Around Budget Constraints</h2>

<h3>Outline of strategy</h3>

<p>Frame the budget constraint as an <strong>objective external limitation</strong>, not a negotiation tactic:</p>
<ul>
    <li>“We are operating within fixed public-sector funding constraints.”</li>
    <li>“The £50,000 represents the maximum legally usable funds available.”</li>
</ul>

<p>Reinforce:</p>
<ul>
    <li>The goal is not to undervalue the consultant’s work</li>
    <li>But to <strong>find a structure that fits within funding reality</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2>Strategy Point 5: Use Issue Packaging and Trade-Offs</h2>

<h3>Outline of strategy</h3>

<p>Offer structured trade-offs:</p>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Reduce scope</strong> in exchange for staying within budget</li>
    <li><strong>Extend timeline</strong> to reduce intensity and cost</li>
    <li><strong>Limit training</strong> in exchange for full system delivery</li>
    <li><strong>Offer future work</strong> in exchange for reduced initial pricing</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Example proposal:</strong></p>
<p>If we reduced the training component and extended the timeline, could the full system be delivered within our £50,000 constraint?</p>

<h2>Strategy Point 6: Apply Conditional Concessions</h2>

<h3>Outline of strategy</h3>

<ul>
    <li>If we commit to future work, then could you reduce the initial fee?</li>
    <li>If we pay part of the cost next year, then could the full scope be maintained?</li>
    <li>If we accept a phased approach, then could we remain within £50,000 now?</li>
</ul>

<p>Ensure:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Every concession is exchanged for value</li>
    <li>No unilateral movement on price</li>
</ul>

<h2>Strategy Point 7: Use Objective Criteria to Justify Constraints</h2>

<h3>Outline of strategy</h3>

<ul>
    <li>Public-sector budgeting rules</li>
    <li>Internal funding approval processes</li>
    <li>Comparison with other departmental spending limits</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Example statement:</strong></p>
<p>“Our budget is constrained by public funding rules, so we need a structure that fits within that framework.”</p>

<h2>Strategy Point 8: Sequencing Using the “Power of Four”</h2>

<h3>Outline of strategy</h3>

<ol>
    <li>Acknowledge price issue early  
        <ul><li>“We need to address the gap between £85k and our £50k limit.”</li></ul>
    </li>
    <li>Shift to needs and priorities</li>
    <li>Explore structured options</li>
    <li>Return to price within structured solution</li>
</ol>

<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<ul>
    <li>Sequence to avoid early stalemate</li>
    <li>Price is the primary sticking point</li>
</ul>

<h2>Strategy Point 9: Risk Management (Client Perspective)</h2>

<h3>Outline of strategy</h3>

<ul>
    <li>Avoid agreeing to <strong>unaffordable commitments</strong></li>
    <li>Ensure <strong>deliverables match expectations</strong></li>
    <li>Clarify what is included vs excluded</li>
    <li>Confirm timeline feasibility</li>
    <li>Confirm <strong>consultant accountability</strong> (SLAs, deliverables)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Strategy Point 10: Relationship and Long-Term Value</h2>

<h3>Outline of strategy</h3>

<ul>
    <li>Emphasise <strong>long-term partnership potential</strong></li>
    <li>Position the project as a <strong>test case for wider rollout</strong></li>
    <li>Offer <strong>non-monetary value</strong>:
        <ul>
            <li>Case study opportunity</li>
            <li>Public-sector entry for TechNova</li>
        </ul>
    </li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Example framing:</strong></p>
<p>“This project could position you strongly for similar work across other school networks.”</p>
<h2>Additional notes:</h2>
<p>If the project is delivered in phases, the first phase must include fully functioning updated software, software implementation and some form of training to staff users of the software.</p>
<p>A successful project for you means an updated system that works as intended and has no bugs, that is implemented into existing systems, and that staff can use.</p>
<p>The highest priority functions are student registration and student records. Timetabling is important but is less of a priority.</p>
<p>Further funding may be available in the next financial period, but you would need to check that, and future funding cannot be guaranteed. Any additional funding requests would need to be approved by senior managers in Nottingham Schools, as you do not have authority to approve budgets.</p>
<p>You would prefer a partnership model option as this would eliminate the need to reduce the scope of the project or the number of deliverables. However, you appreciate that this may not be possible and are willing to explore other options.
</p>
